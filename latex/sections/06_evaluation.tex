\subsection{Calibration}

Reliability diagrams (\cref{fig:calibration}) show that LightGBM and XGBoost are well calibrated in scenario~B (close to the diagonal), whereas Logistic Regression is the worst on both scenarios. ECE (10-bin quantile) is reported in the benchmark JSON: \num{0.0430} for XGB/B vs \num{0.3188} for LogReg/B.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{stop_level/calibration_grid.png}
    \caption{Reliability diagrams (predicted vs empirical fraction). XGB and LightGBM in B follow the perfect-calibration diagonal closely; LogReg is dramatically over-confident at high probability bins.}
    \label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Cascading respect}

A diagnostic specific to scenario B asks: \emph{does the model actually use its inflight signal?} For each model we compute $P(\text{predicted}\mid\text{prev stop late})$ vs $P(\text{predicted}\mid\text{prev stop on time})$. A meaningful gap demonstrates the model is reading the cascading delay. \cref{fig:cascading} shows that the three tree-based models cluster near $\sim$\num{0.97} in the late condition vs $\sim$\num{0.05} in the on-time condition; LogReg shows a softer gap (\num{0.65} vs \num{0.05}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{stop_level/cascading_respect.png}
    \caption{Scenario~B only: $P(\text{predicted disrupted}\mid\text{prev stop late})$ vs $P(\text{predicted disrupted}\mid\text{prev stop on time})$. Tree models show a~$\sim$0.95 gap; LogReg is softer.}
    \label{fig:cascading}
\end{figure}
