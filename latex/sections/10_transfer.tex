\section{Cross-country transfer and failure modes}
\label{sec:transfer}

\subsection{Setup}

The cause classifier is trained on Dutch labels because Italy and Finland do not publish equivalent cause data. To assess whether a model trained on Dutch operations generalises across borders, we apply the winning Dutch classifier -- with no fine-tuning -- to all disrupted Italian stops (\num{340319} rows) and all disrupted Finnish stops (\num{865560} rows). The output is a predicted cause distribution; we audit it against published domain knowledge of each country's railway operations.

\subsection{Predicted distributions}

\Cref{tab:transfer} reports the predicted cause distributions on Italian and Finnish disrupted stops. Mean confidence (the average predicted probability of the chosen class) is \num{0.600} on Italy and \num{0.369} on Finland. Confidence on Italy is high but for the wrong reasons: the model collapses onto \emph{engineering work}, a class on which it scores F1$=$\num{0.000} within the Netherlands.

\subsection{Italy: mode collapse onto \emph{engineering work}}

\SI{54.8}{\percent} of Italian disrupted stops are predicted as \emph{engineering work}. Within the Netherlands this class reaches F1$=$\num{0.000} on the held-out test (\cref{tab:cause-perclass}); the Italian prediction is therefore a transfer artefact, not real signal. Two mechanisms compound:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Italian \texttt{temperature} column is all zero.} The Phase~1 helper \texttt{lib\_italy.py} zero-fills missing weather columns instead of preserving \texttt{NaN}. The cause classifier trained on real Dutch temperatures interprets a column of zeros as ``extreme winter conditions'', a pattern the model had learned to associate with non-default classes in training.
    \item \textbf{Class-balance pathology under domain shift.} On out-of-distribution data, \texttt{class\_weight='balanced'} causes rare-class up-weighted decision paths to behave as the default fallback when the model is uncertain. \emph{Engineering work} is one such rare class with a distinctive mid-tier signal in the Dutch training set, so it dominates the Italian transfer.
\end{enumerate}

We assign Italy a trust score of \textbf{3/10}: the model is consistent and confident, but on the wrong class.

\subsection{Finland: domain-plausible distribution, but \emph{weather} is still missed}

The Finnish distribution flattens substantially: \emph{infrastructure} \SI{30.2}{\percent}, \emph{external} \SI{30.0}{\percent}, \emph{engineering work} \SI{18.5}{\percent}, \emph{unknown} \SI{13.7}{\percent}. This profile is close to the published Finnish railway literature, which describes infrastructure failures (frozen points, signal icing) as a primary winter cause, with external interference (passers-by, animals on track) and operational scheduling issues following.

\paragraph{But weather is still missed.} Only \SI{0.1}{\percent} of Finnish disrupted stops are predicted as \emph{weather}, even though \SI{27}{\percent} of Finnish stops have \texttt{weather\_severity}~$\geq$~3 and the same model achieves F1$=$\num{0.509} on \emph{weather} \emph{within the Netherlands}. Even at the most extreme severity bucket -- severity~$=$~4 with median snow depth at least \SI{25}{cm}, roughly \num{277000} stops in our window -- only \SI{0.7}{\percent} are tagged weather.

The root cause is a feature-scale mismatch. The Dutch \texttt{precipitation} column comes from Open-Meteo daily aggregates measured in millimetres per day. The Finnish \texttt{precipitation} column comes from FMI per-stop hourly observations measured in millimetres per hour. The two are on numerically incompatible scales. The threshold the model learned for ``precipitation $\Rightarrow$ weather cause'' on Dutch values does not fire on Finnish values, because the Finnish per-hour numbers are systematically smaller. We assign Finland a trust score of \textbf{4/10}: domain-plausible everywhere except weather, which is the one class Finland is most likely to need.

\subsection{Recommended next steps}

The numbers above describe a partial success. To make the transfer operationally usable we recommend the following steps, in order of expected lift.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Per-country mean-variance alignment of the temperature and precipitation columns before transfer (CORAL or a simpler standardisation).
    \item Fix the Italian zero-fill weather bug (\texttt{lib\_italy.py:204}) and re-run; this is a single-line change with potentially the largest impact.
    \item Hand-coded deterministic rules for the high-confidence cases. The simplest version: \texttt{weather\_severity}~$\geq$~3 and \texttt{snow\_depth}~$\geq$~15\,cm $\Rightarrow$ \emph{weather}.
    \item Hierarchical modelling: predict \texttt{cause\_group} first, then a sub-cause within group.
    \item Eventually, collect a small per-country labelled set (5--10\,K rows) and fine-tune locally.
\end{enumerate}
