\section{SHAP explainability}
\label{sec:xai}

\subsection{Setup}

We pick the strongest tabular model per scenario by test PR-AUC -- XGBoost wins both -- and run \emph{TreeSHAP}~\cite{lundberg2017unified,lundberg2020local} on the \emph{full} 2.30~M-row test set, not a sample. SHAP value tensors of shape $(2{,}303{,}236 \times F)$ are persisted as \texttt{shap\_values.npy} immediately after compute. The whole Phase~4 pipeline (both scenarios + retrains for ablation + 14 figures) runs in $\sim$34 minutes.

The pre-departure model relies most on \emph{historical reliability of this specific (train, station) pair}: \texttt{train\_station\_lag7\_rate} = \num{1.048} mean $|\text{SHAP}|$, followed by \texttt{avg\_historical\_delay} (\num{0.616}), \texttt{stop\_order} (\num{0.245}), \texttt{cum\_distance\_km} (\num{0.160}) and \texttt{temperature} (\num{0.156}). The inflight model is dominated by \texttt{prev\_stop\_actual\_delay} (\num{2.547}) -- nearly $2.4\times$ the next strongest feature.

\cref{fig:beeswarm} shows the per-row SHAP distributions coloured by feature value. The scenario-B \texttt{prev\_stop\_actual\_delay} row spans \([-2,~+9]\) SHAP units with red (high) values clearly pushing the prediction strongly toward ``disrupted'' -- the textbook ``inflight cliff''.

\subsection{Lag-feature ablation}

The lag-feature ablation re-fits the winning model with all \texttt{*\_lag*} and inflight features removed (keeping only static, weather and position). The PR-AUC drops:

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Lag-feature ablation. The lag and inflight features carry virtually all the test-set signal in scenario~B.}
    \label{tab:lag-ablation}
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
        \toprule
        Scenario & PR-AUC (full) & PR-AUC (no lag) & $\Delta$ \\
        \midrule
        A & 0.552 & 0.355 & $+\,0.197$ \\
        B & 0.952 & 0.355 & $+\,0.597$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\Cref{fig:lag-ablation} visualises the bar drop. Without lag features, both scenarios collapse to the same \num{0.355} baseline -- a static-features-only model that's barely better than the disruption rate. The whole A$\to$B uplift is carried by lag and inflight features.

\subsection{Local explanations}

We pick four prototypical rows from the test set -- highest-confidence true positive, worst false negative, confident false positive, and the row where the A$\leftrightarrow$B prediction flipped most dramatically -- and produce SHAP waterfall plots (\cref{fig:waterfalls}). The TP plot for service \texttt{FI\_28\_2024-06-30} at station \texttt{FI\_HKH} shows \texttt{prev\_stop\_actual\_delay}=35\,min contributing $+8.43$ SHAP units alone, dwarfing every other feature. The model correctly predicts disruption with $p=1.000$.

\subsection{Scenario uplift across stop positions}

The scenario uplift map (\cref{fig:uplift}) plots $P_B - P_A$ per row against \texttt{position\_norm}. Mean uplift = $-\num{0.115}$: counter-intuitively, scenario~B \emph{lowers} most predictions because it is much more selective. Scenario~A over-predicts (more false positives); scenario~B uses the inflight signal to rule them out. The uplift is more negative for late-route positions (\texttt{position\_norm}~$>$~0.75) where the inflight evidence is most informative.
